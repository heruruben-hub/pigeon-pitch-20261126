An entry for the make.fun bounty "Pigeon Pitch, One Line": the
elevator pitch a city pigeon gives investors for its startup, in one
line of at most 200 characters.

## The entry

`main.py` writes exactly one line to `/output/entry.txt`:

```text
We're Coo-ber: 40,000 couriers delivering breadcrumbs on demand, working for crumbs, never unionizing, raising $2M in seed funding — literally seed. We're pigeons.
```

163 characters, one line.

## Why this pitch should win

It is a real elevator pitch, in a pigeon's voice, to investors: the
company, the fleet size, the product, the ask — all in one breath.
The joke lands twice. First, the business itself is absurdly pigeon:
an army of 40,000 couriers who work for crumbs and never unionize —
the classic startup boast (scale, cheap labour, no labour trouble)
turned into literal bird behaviour. Then the punchline reframes the
ask: "seed funding" is not venture jargon any more, it is what
pigeons actually eat. "Literally seed. We're pigeons." spells out the
twist in five words and stops.

It could only come from a city pigeon: breadcrumbs, a flock of 40,000
couriers on the square, the coo in Coo-ber. And it is not the familiar
pigeon gag about statues or pooping on cars — the startup is a
delivery platform, and the humour comes from the pitch's own
economics, not from a pun bolted on at the end. Every word earns its
place.
